01Dream Strokes

Art anyone can join

Way in materials, a story, a shared table

  1. Creative

    I started Dream Strokes in April 2025 because there were few places where anyone could simply come and make art with others, especially without paying for a class. Each session brings the materials, a story to draw into and facilitators, so taking part needs no skill and no equipment of your own. It began in shelters in Ho Chi Minh City and later reached care homes for older adults.

  2. Expressive

    Our lesson plans give children another way to say how they see things. In the Magic Mirror, each child draws the person they see in a paper mirror frame: one girl drew herself in a crown, one boy spinning a basketball. When we swapped free drawing for story worlds, the children who had said least changed most. My guess is that a character gave them someone to speak through.

  3. Connection

    In the first sessions every child had their own box of crayons and drew mostly alone. The next time, we placed one tray in the middle. Soon hands were crossing the table, and children were deciding together which green belonged to the grass. Sessions now end on one piece the whole table made. Through OPEN, our concerts, football tournaments and exhibition, more than 1,700 people from outside the shelters have joined in.

50 workshops · ~200 children · 6 shelters · 2 care homes

02Research

Two ways to say how you feel

Expressive Way in: a format that fits the person

I designed a study with 68 young adults. Each completed two sessions: one working with art materials, one reflecting in a dialogue with AI.

Neither method won. What helped depended partly on how familiar someone already was with art. For some, making something with their hands was an easier place to begin. For others, conversation was.

At TeenCare, a startup building tools for teenagers, I proposed something in the same spirit: letting people meet psychology through a story they can step into.

03Together!

Keeping the days we missed

Connection Way in: seeing what everyone else did

Dream Strokes members live in six cities and provinces, so most of them never saw what the others did: the photograph taken while packing supplies, the voice note on the way home.

With members of the team I built Together!, a shared diary where everyone leaves writing, photographs and voice notes on one timeline. Dream Strokes and more than 20 other projects now keep theirs on it.

04Events

Ten bands, one stage

Gathering Way in: one person moving closer to the stage

I produced Beats of Hope, a benefit concert where more than ten student bands played for over 300 people. It raised about 25 million VND for play and storytelling sessions with children in hospital. I have also run a school football league, a water-rocket contest and a science festival.

What I remember most from that night is a moment near the start: one person moved closer to the stage, and then the people around them followed.
